# 18. Regression improvement program — closing the leaderboard gap

**Scope.** Regression (MAE↓) is the single clearest gap to Codabench hidden-val #1 (our **17.18** vs their
**14.74**, `stats/14 §9`) and the one task where the bare `general` adapter beat the deployed regression
specialist (10.96 < 12.70 on true_test, `stats/14 §4`). This ledger holds the experiments that test *why*
and *how* to close it. Narrative + decisions: `PROGRESS.md` 2026-07-24. Motivation / mechanism (task
transfer): `stats/12` (regression = top "sponge"; {counting,regression} = donor pair).

**The four candidate levers (from the 2026-07-24 design):**
1. **Numeric grouped adapter** — one adapter on counting ∪ regression (capture the numeric positive transfer).
2. **CoT for regression** — the counting-CoT win (`stats/4`) applied to measuring.
3. **Boneresorption tail** — the macro is dominated by ~70 hard boneresorption cases.
4. **Strongest `general` variant** — A∪B / all-data general (deferred, low prior — `stats/14 §8` says A∪B general didn't help regression).

## §5 — WHY general beats the regression specialist (PGF + offline mechanism) — DONE 2026-07-24

The paradox: general > specialist (data-starvation intuition) BUT counting-mix (E1) did WORSE. Resolved with
PGF + an offline prediction-distribution diagnostic (no GPU; `scripts/analyze_regression_phase1.py`, commit
`06f1b6f`; true_test regression preds).

**(a) PGF reframe.** `stats/12 §2` regression column (zero-shot transfer INTO regression): counting **+0.38**
(highest single donor), multilabel 0.26, reportgen 0.19, cls 0.18, detection −1.33 (pirate). So counting has
the *highest zero-shot transfer* into regression — yet it's the *worst co-training partner* (E1). **Lesson:
zero-shot PGF transfer ≠ multi-task co-training gain; counting→regression is the counterexample.** general's
win is a **portfolio** (many positive donors) + **volume** effect (58k rows vs E1's 6k), and holds DESPITE
general containing the detection pirate → breadth/volume regularization, not a single magic donor.

**(b) Offline scale-contamination diagnostic** (boneresorption gold mean 37 vs counting gold mean 129; signed
err = pred − gold):

| config | dataset | mean pred | p90 pred | mean signed err | err std | MAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reg specialist | boneresorption (n70) | 26.9 | 43.5 | −10.35 | 26.4 | 22.14 |
| E1 numeric | boneresorption | 32.7 | 67.5 | −4.48 | **31.1** | 23.40 |
| general | boneresorption | 31.2 | 60.2 | −6.07 | **23.8** | **18.93** |
| *(gold)* | boneresorption | — | 74.6 | — | — | — |
| E1 numeric | iugc (n516) | 87.3 | 111 | +0.01 | 5.43 | 3.13 |
| reg specialist | iugc | 86.9 | 106 | −0.31 | 5.31 | 3.27 |

- On **boneresorption** (scale-mismatched to counting): E1 is higher-magnitude (p90 67.5 vs spec 43.5) AND
  **noisier** (err std +18%, IQR +22%). NOT systematic over-prediction (all configs net under, %over ~flat) →
  the damage is **variance / fat-tail inflation**, not a mean upward shift.
- On **iugc** (scale-MATCHED to counting): **zero** contamination signature, E1 ≈ spec. Dataset-specificity is
  exactly what scale-contamination predicts.

**(c) Mechanism synthesis — the answer to "is regression data-starved?"** The specialist's core failure is
**tail under-prediction / mean-reversion** on the hard high boneresorption cases (p90 pred 43.5 ≪ gold 74.6,
mean signed err −10.4): with ~5k rows and a 70-case tail it plays safe. **general fixes it with CALIBRATED
higher-tail predictions** (p90 60.2, lowest err std 23.8, best MAE 18.9). **E1 also breaks mean-reversion but
UNCALIBRATED** (p90 67.5, err std 31.1) → higher values on wrong cases → worse. **So: YES regression is
starved — specifically tail-CALIBRATION-starved — and the cure is BREADTH/VOLUME (general's diluted, diverse
magnitude signal), NOT adjacent-task content (counting fills it toxically via output-scale conflict).** That is
why general helps and counting-mixing hurts.
